## What is already in place

The sound card is illustrated today: it carries the schwa's own articulation
plate, `img/articulation/026-vowel-schwa.jpg`, cropped to the vocal tract
beside a large `/ə/`. That is the right picture for a panel headed "Meet the

## What is missing

**The sofa.** The handoff asks that a correct answer reveal "a checkmark, the
sofa illustration, and 'You heard /ə/ at the end of sofa.'" The checkmark and
the line are there. There is no sofa, and nothing in the app stands in for
one, so the reveal is currently type and a tick — not a faded box, not a
placeholder.

## The thing worth deciding first

The exercise runs **four** rounds, not one: sofa, machine, station, away. A
sofa drawn for round one leaves three rounds bare, which will look like the
art is missing rather than deliberately absent.

Two honest ways out, and I would take the first:

1. **No spot art in the reveal at all.** Keep the tick and the sentence for
   every round, so the four are consistent and nothing looks half-finished.
   The sofa then belongs on the lesson card in the course list instead, where
   it is decoration rather than a promise.
2. **Four vignettes** — sofa, a machine, a railway station, a path leading
   away. More of your time, and three of the four are harder to draw small
   than a sofa is.

Say which and I will wire it; either is a data change, not a code change.
